The photovoltaic effect

From photons to useful electrical power.

A solar cell is a semiconductor device that converts incident light into electrical energy. When suitable photons are absorbed, they can create electron–hole pairs. The device structure separates these charges and allows current to flow through an external circuit.

01

Light absorption

Photons with sufficient energy are absorbed by the semiconductor, exciting electrons across its band gap.

02

Charge separation

An internal electric field or selective contacts help separate electrons and holes before they recombine.

03

Current and voltage

Contacts collect the charges. A connected circuit allows current to flow and delivers electrical power to a load.

Materials and device physics

Essential properties of solar cells.

Material composition, the band gap, charge transport, interfaces, and device design all influence how efficiently a solar cell converts light.

◈

Semiconductor Material

Silicon, CdTe, CIGS, and perovskite absorbers have different electronic and optical properties.

λ

Band Gap

The band gap influences which photon energies can be absorbed and the voltage a cell can deliver.

↯

Light Absorption

Absorption depends on the material's optical properties, thickness, and the spectrum of incident light.

⇄

Charge Transport

Carrier mobility, lifetime, recombination, and contact selectivity affect collection of photogenerated charges.

◉

Thermal Stability

Temperature affects voltage, efficiency, and long-term material stability; most silicon cells lose voltage as they heat up.

⌁

Durability

Encapsulation, moisture resistance, UV exposure, and mechanical strength influence module service life.

η = (Pmax / Pin) × 100%

Measure the performance

Electrical characteristics explained.

The current–voltage curve reveals how a solar cell behaves under illumination. Its key parameters help researchers compare devices and identify performance losses.

Parameter Meaning
Short-circuit current (Isc) Current at zero terminal voltage.
Open-circuit voltage (Voc) Voltage at zero output current.
Maximum power (Pmax) Highest product of current and voltage.
Fill factor (FF) Ratio of maximum power to IscVoc.
Efficiency (η) Electrical output divided by incident light power.
Temperature coefficient Change in a performance parameter per degree.

Exact values depend on technology, cell design, temperature, and measurement conditions.

The equations behind performance

Electrical power is the product of terminal voltage and current. At the maximum power point, this product is at its highest value.

Electrical power P = V × I
Maximum power Pmax = VmpImp
Fill factor FF = Pmax/(VocIsc)
Efficiency η = Pmax/Pin
Remember: a higher short-circuit current or open-circuit voltage alone does not necessarily mean higher efficiency. The maximum power point, incident optical power, and measurement conditions matter.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions.

What is a solar cell?

A solar cell is a semiconductor device that converts light into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect.

What is the difference between a solar cell and a solar panel?

A solar cell is an individual photovoltaic device. A solar panel or module connects multiple cells and packages them for practical use.

Why does a solar cell produce less voltage when it gets hotter?

For conventional silicon cells, increasing temperature generally increases the saturation current and reduces open-circuit voltage. The resulting power loss depends on the cell's temperature coefficients and operating conditions.

What do the I–V and P–V curves show?

The I–V curve shows current as a function of voltage. The P–V curve shows output power as a function of voltage and helps identify the maximum power point.

What is the maximum power point?

It is the operating point at which the product of current and voltage is greatest under a given set of conditions. Maximum power point tracking (MPPT) electronics help a system operate near this point.

Can solar panels work on cloudy days?

Yes. They can produce electricity from diffuse sunlight, but their output is usually lower than under strong direct sunlight.

Are the graphs on this website experimental measurements?

No. They are generated from a simplified illustrative model for learning purposes. Real measurements depend on the cell technology, irradiance, temperature, device area, and test equipment.
