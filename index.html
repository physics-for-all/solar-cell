01 — CONTEXTWHY SOLAR?

A different relationship with energy.

Photovoltaics make electricity from a renewable resource without direct fuel combustion at the point of generation. Their value extends beyond the panel itself.

Energy is not only about how much we produce. It is about how we produce it.

Solar cells can be installed near the places where electricity is needed, expanded as demand changes, and combined with batteries, grid infrastructure, and other renewable sources. Their total environmental impact also depends on manufacturing, transport, installation, and end-of-life management.

02 — MECHANISMTHE PHYSICS

One photon. A chain of events.

Photovoltaic conversion depends on the interaction of light with semiconductor electronic states and on the device's ability to collect the generated charge.

FIG. 002 / SEMICONDUCTOR JUNCTION · CONCEPTUAL DIAGRAM

The photovoltaic effect, explained.

A semiconductor absorbs a photon when its energy is sufficient for an allowed electronic transition. This can generate an electron and a hole. The cell's internal fields and selective contacts help extract these carriers before they recombine.

01

Absorption

Incident photons transfer energy to the semiconductor's electronic system.

02

Generation and separation

Electron–hole pairs form; junction fields and selective layers promote charge separation.

03

Collection

Electrical contacts collect carriers, allowing current to flow through an external circuit.

04

Useful power

A load receives electrical power determined by the operating current and voltage.

03 — MATERIALSDEVICE DESIGN

Performance starts at the material level.

Optical absorption alone is not enough. A successful photovoltaic device must also separate, transport, and collect charges while limiting energy losses.

P / 01

Band gap

Sets the minimum photon energy for band-to-band absorption and strongly influences the voltage-current trade-off.

P / 02

Absorption

Determines how much incident light is absorbed across the device's active layer.

P / 03

Carrier mobility

Influences how easily electrons and holes move through a material under an electric field.

P / 04

Recombination

Charge carriers lost before collection reduce current, voltage, and conversion efficiency.

P / 05

Interfaces

Contacts and layer boundaries can improve selectivity or introduce barriers and defects.

P / 06

Stability

Heat, moisture, oxygen, UV exposure, and mechanical stress affect long-term performance.

Conversion efficiency

Electrical output / incident optical power
η = Pmax / Pin × 100%
Pmax = maximum electrical output power · Pin = incident optical power on the cell

08 — PERSPECTIVETRADE-OFFS

Good engineering starts with honest limits.

Solar energy is a powerful part of the energy transition, but real installations must account for variability, materials, system design and end-of-life treatment.

What works

  • +Renewable source with no direct combustion during generation.
  • +Modular architecture that can be expanded to meet demand.
  • +Low routine maintenance for many properly installed systems.
  • +Can be combined with storage, demand management and grid power.

What to consider

  • !Output changes with sunlight, shading, weather and time.
  • !Heat commonly lowers the operating voltage of silicon cells.
  • !Manufacturing requires materials, energy and industrial infrastructure.
  • !Recycling and responsible end-of-life management remain important.
09 — FIELD QUESTIONSFAQ

Questions worth asking.

Short explanations of the central ideas behind photovoltaic devices and their performance.

What is a solar cell?

A solar cell is a semiconductor device that converts incident light into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect.

How is a cell different from a module?

A cell is an individual photovoltaic device. A module, often called a solar panel, combines cells and packages them for practical use.

Why does temperature affect efficiency?

For conventional silicon cells, higher temperature generally reduces open-circuit voltage. The net change in power depends on the device's temperature coefficients and operating conditions.

What do I–V and P–V curves tell us?

The I–V curve describes current as a function of voltage. The P–V curve plots electrical power against voltage and shows the maximum power point.

What is MPPT?

Maximum power point tracking is a control method used by many solar inverters and charge controllers to keep a photovoltaic system operating near its maximum-power point.

Can solar cells work on cloudy days?

Yes. Cells can convert diffuse daylight, although output is usually lower than under strong direct sunlight.
